## 8. Method notes — what to carry into the next refresh

1. **Verify prescriptions, not just findings.** Where the register is wrong it is almost always the
   *prescription*, not the diagnosis. GAP-037 prescribes an edit already made; GAP-019c calls "no code" on a
   shipped endpoint. The plan already recorded this once (*"five of eight prescriptions executed were wrong as
   written"*) — this refresh confirms it as systematic, not incidental.
2. **Count by classification, never by grep.** The token grep both over- and under-counts (§2).
3. **A stale comment is a first-class defect.** Two of the most expensive items here — the notification-rewire
   epic and GAP-029's dormant workflow engine — persisted *because a comment justified them*. Neither would have
   survived a reader who checked the code.
4. **An auditor's incidental claims are not evidence either.** Two auditors in this refresh disagreed on whether
   `Feedback360IntegrationTests.cs` is Testcontainers or InMemory (it is **InMemory**, `:61`). The same rule the
   gap analysis applies to ledgers applies to its own agents.
5. **Nothing here was executed.** The claimed-broken screens (#1, #4, #5, #6, #7) are static inferences at
   85–95% confidence. **One `/test-us US-PRF-002` and a Playwright pass over `/employees/:id`, the LOP screen and
   the recommendation workspace would settle all five in minutes** — do that before sizing the work.
